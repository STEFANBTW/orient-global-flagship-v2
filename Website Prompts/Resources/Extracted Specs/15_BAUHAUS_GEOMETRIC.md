# 15. BAUHAUS / GEOMETRIC

## 1. Ranked Industries
- **Architecture & Industrial Design Firms**: Bauhaus was literally the school of architecture and industrial design; this is its ancestral territory
- **Graphic Design & Branding Studios**: the Bauhaus principle of design as a discipline with rules and theory is the operating philosophy of serious design studios
- **Art Museums & Cultural Institutions**: particularly those with modernist or contemporary collections
- **Premium Furniture & Product Design Brands**: the form-follows-function philosophy is the foundation of quality furniture design (Knoll, Herman Miller, Vitsœ)
- **Architectural Education / Design Schools**: these institutions ARE the descendants of the Bauhaus
- **High-End Stationery & Paper Goods**: the rigor of geometric type and composition on premium paper
- **Photography Studios & Agencies**: the compositional rigour of Bauhaus translates directly to photographic composition
- **Technology Companies (Design-Forward)**: companies that want to signal design seriousness and functional rigor
- **Fashion (Structured, Minimalist)**: fashion brands whose aesthetic is built on strong silhouette and geometric construction
- **Publishing Houses (Avant-Garde / Academic)**: design theory publications and avant-garde literary presses
- **The Deterministic Application Guide**: Bauhaus / Geometric

## 2. Why This Aesthetic Works
- **The Bauhaus school (Dessau, Germany, 1919**: 1933) established a revolutionary principle: form follows function. Every design decision must be justified by its functional purpose. Decoration for its own sake is dishonest. The visual result of this philosophy is a design language that communicates absolute intentionality — every element is exactly where it is because it must be there.
This communicates extraordinary competence. When a viewer processes a Bauhaus-influenced design and feels that every element is necessary and precisely placed, they attribute the same precision to the brand itself. The rigor of the design implies rigor in everything the company does.
The use of primary colors and basic geometric forms also works through elemental clarity — the three primary colors and three basic shapes are the irreducible building blocks of visual language. Using them communicates that the brand understands fundamentals completely and has mastered them, rather than hiding behind decorative complexity.

## 3. Color System
### The Palette Logic
- Bauhaus color theory was developed by Josef Albers and Johannes Itten at the school. The system begins with primary colors as the foundation.
- The Bauhaus Primary System
- **Red**: #CC0000 or #D40000 — not the bright red of traffic lights (#FF0000) but a deeper, slightly darker red that references the red of Bauhaus posters and the German expressionist painters who influenced the school
- **Yellow**: #F5C400 or #E8B800 — a warm, golden yellow, not the acid yellow of cyberpunk. References sunlight and the yellow of Bauhaus typography.
- **Blue**: #003B8E or #0040AA — a deep, primary blue. Not navy (too corporate), not royal blue (too generic). The specific blue of Bauhaus graphics.
- **Black**: #111111 — used as the dominant color in many Bauhaus compositions. Black is the color of ink, of type, of the printed line.
- **White**: #FFFFFF or #FAFAFA — the ground against which the primary colors and black are placed.
- Expanded Geometric Palette
- Beyond the pure primaries, Bauhaus-influenced design can expand to include:
- **Deep red-brown**: #8B1A1A — where the primary red meets earth
- **Warm grey**: #8C8C8C — the grey of industrial concrete and machine surfaces
- **Pale yellow**: #FFF4CC — the lightest yellow, used as a background or section fill
- **Dark blue**: #001F5B — the darkest possible blue before it becomes black
- How to Use These Colors
Bauhaus uses primary colors as graphic elements, not just as UI signals. A large red rectangle is a design element. A black circle is a design element. These geometric color-shapes are placed with compositional purpose:
- **Primary color**: fills large geometric shape elements that organize the composition
- **Secondary (black and white)**: provides the ground and the contrast
- **No gradients**: the flat, pure color of Bauhaus is as important as the shape

## 4. Typography
### The Core Philosophy
- **Bauhaus typography was revolutionary**: the school rejected the decorative, historical typefaces of 19th-century printing and demanded that type be designed according to the same geometric, functional principles as everything else.
### Font Classification
- The Geometric Construction Fonts
- **Futura**: designed by Paul Renner in 1927, directly influenced by Bauhaus principles. Letterforms are constructed from circles, triangles, and straight lines. Every letter reduces to its geometric essence. The definitive Bauhaus-era font.
- **Bauhaus 93**: a direct digital revival of the typeface designed at the Bauhaus school itself. Heavy, rounded, constructed from circles and straight lines. Use for display headlines.
- **ITC Avant Garde Gothic**: designed by Herb Lubalin, directly referencing geometric modernism. Distinctive circular letterforms.
- **Raleway**: a free alternative with strong geometric construction.
- **Josefin Sans**: a free font with explicit Bauhaus construction logic — its letterforms are clearly built on a geometric grid.
- Why Geometric Fonts?
Because the Bauhaus principle is that letterforms, like products and buildings, should be constructed from rational geometric principles rather than inherited historical conventions. A Futura letterform can be drawn using only a compass and a straight edge — it is geometrically constructible. This construction logic is visible in the final form, and it communicates the same rigor as the Bauhaus design philosophy itself.
### Sizing Scale
- Bauhaus design uses dramatic scale contrast:
- **Hero headline**: 80px to 120px, Bold (700) or Black (900) — the letterforms are themselves geometric compositions at this scale
- **Section headline**: 48px to 64px
- **Sub-heading**: 28px to 36px
- **Body text**: 16px to 18px, Regular (400) — the body should be clean and disappear; the headlines are the visual event
- **Labels**: 11px to 12px, uppercase, Bold (700), wide tracking (0.15em to 0.20em)
- Alignment in Bauhaus Typography
- **Bauhaus typography often uses asymmetric alignment**: a deliberate violation of centered or fully left-aligned convention:
- Text placed at the intersection of geometric color planes
- Vertical text (rotated 90 degrees) used as a secondary headline or label
- Text that begins at an unexpected horizontal position, aligned to the edge of a geometric element rather than to the page margin
- Color of Type
- **Black on white or yellow backgrounds**: maximum contrast, maximum legibility
- White on red or blue backgrounds
- The primary accent color on white backgrounds for specific emphasis
- **Never grey body text in Bauhaus design**: the philosophy demands clarity, and grey type is a concession to visual comfort over clarity

## 5. Spacing & Layout
- **The Bauhaus Grid**: The Most Rigorous of All Aesthetics
- **Bauhaus layout is grid-based to an extreme**: but the grid is the composition, not just the organizing structure. The grid lines are visible (as thin black lines or as edges of colored planes) and the content sits within the grid cells as precisely as a window sits within a building's structural frame.
- The Grid as Composition
- Rather than a 12-column grid used invisibly (as in flat design or corporate), Bauhaus uses the grid as a visible compositional element:
- The page is divided into primary horizontal and vertical bands
- Large colored rectangles fill entire grid cells
- Typography sits within cells, aligned to the cell edges
- **Negative space is also a cell**: an empty grid cell is as deliberate as a filled one
- Section Construction
- Each section of a Bauhaus-designed page is constructed as a composition of:
- A large flat-color geometric element (a full-width red band, a large blue square, a black diagonal line)
- The primary content (headline, body text) placed in geometric relationship to element 1
- An optional secondary geometric element (a smaller circle, a thin line, a contrasting color block)
### Spacing
- **Within each compositional cell**: the padding is generous (40px to 64px) to allow each element clarity
- **Between major sections**: clean breaks — either a change in background color (from white to yellow, from yellow to black) or a thin 1px to 2px horizontal line
- **No curves in the layout structure**: every spacing boundary is a straight horizontal or vertical line

## 6. Borders, Shapes & Forms
- The Geometric Shape System
This is the most distinctive visual element of Bauhaus design. The three primary shapes — circle, triangle, and square — are used as active design elements throughout the composition:
- The Circle
- **Used as**: background graphic element, image frame, icon container, accent bullet
- **Color**: filled with a primary color or left as a stroke-only outline
- **Size**: either very large (600px to 800px diameter, partially off-screen) or very small (8px to 16px as bullet points)
- The circle is associated with yellow in Bauhaus theory (Itten's color-form associations)
- The Triangle
- **Used as**: directional indicators, accent shapes, compositional balancing elements
- **Color**: red (Itten's association of red with the triangle)
- Often rotated to various angles as a compositional element
- The Square / Rectangle
- **Used as**: the primary organizational unit of the grid, colored background bands, content containers
- **Color**: blue (Itten's association of blue with the square)
- **The most commonly used shape**: it IS the grid
### Border Radius
- **0px on all geometric elements**: the geometry is pure and uncompromised. Rounded shapes are circles (0px radius is not applied — the shape is constructed as a circle, not as a rounded square). Every rectangle is a rectangle, not a rounded rectangle.
- Line Work
- **Bauhaus design uses constructed lines**: single black lines at 2px to 4px weight — as active compositional elements:
- Horizontal rules of exactly the column width, ending at precise grid points
- Diagonal lines (used sparingly) creating dynamic movement in otherwise static compositions
- Lines that extend from the edge of a text block to the edge of the column — creating precise spatial relationships

## 7. UI Components
### Navigation
- Bauhaus navigation is as structured and geometric as a building plan:
- A thick horizontal band (48px to 64px) in black or a primary color runs full-width across the top
- The logo/wordmark sits in a grid-aligned position within this band — left-aligned or centered, in white or contrasting type
- Navigation links sit within the band, right-aligned, in white (on dark band) or the band's contrasting color
- **The band has a hard, geometric bottom edge**: no shadow, no gradient, just the precise edge of a colored plane against the page
### Buttons
- **Flat-color rectangles with 0px border radius**: precise geometric shapes
- **Primary**: solid red, white text. No border-radius. No shadow.
- **Secondary**: solid black, white text. Or white with 2px black border, black text.
- **Hover**: the button changes to the next primary color (red becomes yellow becomes blue) — the Bauhaus color cycle
- **Transition**: 150ms, instant enough to feel responsive, slow enough to be perceived
### Cards
- **Flat rectangles with no border-radius, no shadow**: they sit on the page as colored geometric planes
- **Alternatively**: content within a thin 2px black-bordered rectangle — the frame is the card boundary
- **No internal background color variation**: the card IS a colored rectangle or a bordered white rectangle

## 8. Imagery & Texture
### Photography
- Photography in Bauhaus design must be geometrically composed:
- Strong horizontal and vertical structure within the frame
- Photographs often converted to high-contrast black and white — removing color focuses attention on form and geometry
- **When in color**: the photography is used in conjunction with flat color overlays — a photo beneath a solid primary color at 30% to 50% opacity, creating a tinted, unified image that integrates with the flat color palette
- Zero Texture
Bauhaus surfaces are flat, pure, and perfectly smooth. The machine aesthetic that inspired Bauhaus production values — clean, precise, industrial — has no room for handmade texture. The clean flatness is the integrity of the design.

## 9. Animation & Motion
- Bauhaus animation is geometric and rule-governed:
- Appropriate animations:
- **Geometric shape transitions**: a circle that morphs into a square, or a colored rectangle that slides across the screen to reveal content — the shapes themselves are the animation subjects
- **Grid-reveal**: the page builds itself section by section from a grid, each cell filling in with its color or content as the user scrolls — making the grid structure visible in time as well as space
- **Strict axis movement**: elements slide horizontally or vertically only — never diagonally, never rotating (except the triangle, which may rotate as a directional indicator)
- **Color transitions**: elements cycle through the primary colors on interaction (red → yellow → blue) — the color theory of the Bauhaus made interactive
- What to strictly avoid:
- **Organic curves in motion paths**: all movement follows straight geometric paths
- **Soft easing that curves the motion invisibly**: Bauhaus motion can use easing at the start and end, but the primary motion path is straight and geometric
- **Decorative particle effects or ambient motion**: Bauhaus design does not have ambience; it has structure

## 10. Master Avoids List
- **Rounded corners on any element that is not a circle**: a rounded rectangle is a compromise and Bauhaus does not compromise geometry
- **Gradients**: Bauhaus color is flat, pure, and unmixed. The blend of two colors produces a third; in Bauhaus theory, you use the three primaries, not the infinite combinations between them.
- **Decorative or expressive typography**: all type is geometrically constructed. A handwritten font is the antithesis of Bauhaus principles.
- **Asymmetric border radii**: if a border-radius exists, it is perfectly uniform on all corners
- **Drop shadows**: they imply that objects cast light in inconsistent directions. Bauhaus design is flat. Flat objects have no shadow.
- **More than three or four colors**: the Bauhaus system uses the three primaries plus black and white. Adding more colors introduces the kind of decorative complexity Bauhaus rejected.
- **Decorative illustration**: all visual elements are geometric constructions. Organic, representational, or decorative illustration violates the geometric purity.
