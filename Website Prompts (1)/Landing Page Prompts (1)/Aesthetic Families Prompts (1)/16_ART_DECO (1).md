The Deterministic Application Guide: Art Deco

### WHY ART DECO WORKS — THE PSYCHOLOGICAL FOUNDATION

Art Deco emerged in the 1920s as the design expression of a specific cultural moment: the post-WWI optimism, the Jazz Age prosperity, the fascination with speed, modernity, and the machine. It fused the ornamental richness of previous decorative traditions with the geometric precision of modernism — creating a style that was simultaneously luxurious and progressive.
The psychological power of Art Deco today is primarily romantic nostalgia — a yearning for a specific imagined golden age of glamour, craftsmanship, and elegance. The Chrysler Building, the Savoy cocktail bar, a Great Gatsby ballroom — these are objects of cultural desire precisely because they represent a version of luxury that feels lost. Art Deco design activates this desire.
It also communicates crafted permanence. Art Deco's geometric precision suggests that every element was calculated and executed with extraordinary care — the kind of care that produced the intricate bronze elevator doors of the Chrysler Building or the geometric inlay of an Art Deco jewellery piece. The ornamental precision signals that this is not mass-produced.

### COLOR

#### The Palette Logic

Art Deco's palette is built on a specific relationship between a dark, rich base and a small number of metallic and jewel-toned accents.
The Primary Art Deco Color Architecture
Dark Foundation Colors:

* Deep black: `#111111` — the primary anchoring color. Art Deco's darkest backgrounds and boldest type.
* Deep navy: `#0A0D2B` or `#0D1140` — the alternative to black, slightly richer
* Ivory/cream: `#F5EED0` or `#EEE5C0` — the warm light ground, referencing the cream of the paper and fabric of the era

Metallic Accent Colors:

* Gold: `#C9A84C` or `#B8860B` or `#D4A929` — the most important Art Deco color. The gold of gilt bronze, of gold-leaf lettering, of costume jewellery. It appears on every Art Deco design — in borders, in ornamental details, in headlines.
* Silver/Platinum: `#C0C0C0`, `#D8D8D8`, `#B0B8C0` — secondary metallic, cooler and more restrained than gold
* Chrome: `#A8B0B8` — the cold, polished metal of the machine age

Jewel Accent Colors (one or two per design):

* Emerald green: `#0A5C3C` or `#0D6B45` — the green of precious stones, peacock feathers, and 1920s evening wear
* Sapphire blue: `#0A2D6B` or `#0D3580` — the deep, gemstone blue
* Ruby red: `#8B1A2A` or `#7A1520` — the deep red of garnets and rubies
* Amethyst: `#4A1570` or `#5C1A8C` — the purple of royalty and Art Deco jewellery

Palette Construction Rule

Dark foundation: 60% to 70% of the composition (black or navy)
* **Gold/metallic: 20% to 25%** — borders, ornamental elements, headlines
* **Jewel tone accent: 5% to 10%** — maximum one or two elements of this color per section

* **The gold must appear everywhere in small doses** — it is the connective tissue of Art Deco design. Without gold, Art Deco loses its defining characteristic.

### TYPOGRAPHY

The Core Philosophy

Art Deco typography is characterized by geometric construction, dramatic height, extreme decorative quality, and a sense of ceremonial formality. The letterforms reference the architectural details of Art Deco buildings — tall, thin proportions with precisely geometric ornamental details.

#### Font Classification

Geometric Display Serifs (The Primary Art Deco Type)

Poiret One: extremely thin, delicate, with Art Deco geometric construction. Named for the fashion designer Paul Poiret, whose era defined Art Deco fashion.
Josefin Slab: combines geometric sans-serif construction with slab serifs — referencing the architectural typography of the 1920s
Playfair Display at display sizes: the high contrast thick-thin strokes reference the engraved typography of Art Deco stationery
Cormorant Garamond Display: at large sizes, the extreme contrast of strokes references engraved metalwork

Geometric Sans-Serifs (For Secondary Text)

* **Futura (see Bauhaus section)** — Futura was designed in 1927, in the heart of the Art Deco era. Its geometric construction references the same formal principles as Art Deco architecture.
Gill Sans: 1928. The British counterpart to Futura. Used for body text and secondary headlines.

Characteristic Art Deco Typographic Techniques

All-caps with wide tracking: headlines in all capitals with tracking of 0.25em to 0.5em. This extreme spacing gives headlines the quality of engraved lettering on a building facade.
Centered alignment: Art Deco typography is almost always centered — it references the symmetry of architectural facades
Gold letterforms: headline text in the gold color, against the dark background
Inline letterforms: type where each stroke has a thin center line in a lighter color — simulating the highlight catch on a polished metal letter

#### Sizing Scale

Hero headline: 64px to 96px, all-caps, wide tracking (0.3em to 0.5em), in gold
Sub-headline: 24px to 36px, all-caps, moderate tracking (0.15em to 0.25em)
Body text: 15px to 17px in the body serif, in cream or near-white on dark backgrounds
Labels and details: 10px to 12px, all-caps, widest tracking (0.3em to 0.4em)

### SPACING & LAYOUT

The Core Philosophy

Art Deco layout is strictly symmetrical. Every element mirrors its counterpart across a central vertical axis. This symmetry references the facades of Art Deco buildings — symmetrical compositions of geometric ornament flanking a central entrance or window.
Symmetry as Absolute Rule

Every section is centered
Flanking decorative elements (the ornamental borders, the geometric motifs) appear in pairs — one on each side of the central content
Headline text is centered
Even the navigation: the logo is centered, with navigation links equally distributed on each side

Ornamental Framing

Sections are framed by ornamental borders constructed from repeating geometric motifs:

Running patterns of chevrons, sunbursts, or fan shapes along horizontal borders
Thin double-line borders (two parallel lines with a small gap between) framing content areas
Corner ornaments at the intersection of horizontal and vertical borders — small geometric devices (diamonds, stylized flowers, cross-forms)

#### Section Spacing

Between sections: marked by a full-width ornamental horizontal divider (a thin double line with a centered geometric element at the midpoint)
Within sections: 80px to 120px above and below content — generous, ceremonial spacing that references the grand interior spaces of Art Deco buildings

### BORDERS, SHAPES & FORMS

Ornamental Borders (The Defining Element)

Art Deco borders are the most elaborately specified of any aesthetic. They are not single lines — they are constructed geometric ornaments:
The Double-Line Border: two parallel lines (0.5px each) with a gap of 4px between them — in the gold color. Used as horizontal section dividers and as frames around featured content.
The Sunburst Element: a central point with radiating lines at precise angular intervals — used at the midpoint of horizontal dividers, at corner junctions, and as standalone decorative elements.
The Chevron Running Border: a repeating pattern of V-shapes or zigzags — used along horizontal bands, referencing the geometric textile patterns of the 1920s.
* **The Fan/Scallop Border: repeating arc shapes** — referencing the Art Deco architectural detailing of balustrades, grilles, and elevator doors.
All borders are in the gold color. Silver is used for secondary or lower-hierarchy borders.

#### Border Radius

0px on all rectangular containers. Art Deco geometry is hard-edged. The contrast between the precise straight lines of the structure and the elaborate curves within the ornamental details is itself a characteristic of the style.
Geometric Shape Motifs

Beyond borders, Art Deco uses specific shapes as active design elements:

The stepped pyramid (ziggurat form): a silhouette that steps inward as it rises — references the Chrysler Building's crown and Art Deco skyscraper profiles
The sunburst/starburst: radiating lines from a central point, often used as a background motif at very low opacity (5% to 10%)
The peacock feather: a specific organic form in Art Deco that contrasts with the geometric language — used sparingly as a decorative illustration element
The stylized fan: a geometric approximation of a folding fan, used as a corner ornament or section decoration

### UI COMPONENTS

#### Navigation

Art Deco navigation is ceremonial:

* **A full-width dark band (navy or black)** — 60px to 80px tall
The brand name centered in the band in the display font, gold colored, all-caps, wide-tracked
* **Navigation links on either side of the brand name** — equidistant, gold-colored, all-caps, small size (12px to 13px), extreme tracking (0.3em)
Below the navigation band: a thin double-line ornamental border in gold running the full page width

#### Buttons

* Bordered rectangle with 0px radius — in the gold color (border: 1px solid `#C9A84C`)
Text: all-caps, gold colored, widely tracked
Background: transparent (ghost) or deep dark (black or navy)
Hover: gold fills the button, text changes to black or navy — the gold invasion of the space
* **Transition: 300ms ease** — measured and ceremonial

Cards and Content Panels

Bordered by the double-line Art Deco border in gold
Corner ornaments at each corner of the border
Dark background (matching the page background)
Content inside has generous padding (32px to 48px) — ceremonial space around each piece of information

### IMAGERY & TEXTURE

#### Photography

Converted to duo-tone: one color mapped to the shadows (black or navy) and another mapped to the highlights (gold or cream) — this is the Art Deco photography treatment
Alternatively: black and white photography with gold overlaid on specific highlight areas
Subject matter: architectural details (bronze doors, geometric facades, chandeliers), glamorous figures in 1920s-adjacent styling, jewellery and luxury objects photographed in dramatic light

Illustrated Elements

Art Deco illustration is more prominent than in most aesthetics:

Geometric architectural silhouettes (skylines, building facades)
* **Stylized figurative illustration** — human figures simplified to geometric forms in the style of Art Deco poster art (Cassandre, Erté)
* **Botanical elements rendered geometrically** — a stylized rose where each petal is a perfect geometric form

Textures

Gold foil effect: a metallic texture applied to gold elements — a shifting, reflective surface quality achieved with a subtle animated gradient
Embossed paper: a very subtle texture suggesting heavy, quality paper — the printing surface of Art Deco packaging
Damask pattern: a repeating symmetrical ornamental pattern at very low opacity (5% to 8%) used as a background texture on the dark foundation

### ANIMATION & MOTION

Appropriate animations:

Ornamental reveal: the Art Deco border draws itself on scroll — each line element extends from the center outward, like a pantograph drawing the border in real time
Gold shimmer: a slow, continuous shimmer sweep across gold elements — simulating the catch of light on real gilt surfaces (animation duration: 3 to 5 seconds per sweep)
Centered entrance: content elements fade in and rise from below by 16px, always in centered position — maintaining the symmetry of the layout during animation
Staggered geometric reveal: when multiple elements appear (an ornamental border + a headline + a sub-heading), they appear in strict sequential order with 150ms to 200ms between each — building the composition ceremonially

What to strictly avoid:

Asymmetric animations (an element sliding in from the left with nothing balancing it from the right) — Art Deco is symmetrical even in motion
Glitch effects or any reference to digital corruption — Art Deco is analog and handcrafted in its references
* **Bouncing or spring physics** — the aesthetic is formal and dignified; physical spring simulation is too playful

WHAT TO STRICTLY AVOID IN ART DECO

* **Asymmetric layout** — the fundamental rule of Art Deco composition is symmetry. Any asymmetric section breaks the architectural logic entirely.
* **Missing gold** — Art Deco without gold is not Art Deco. Gold must appear in borders, in headlines, in ornamental details. It is non-optional.
* **Rounded corners** — the precise geometric language of Art Deco has no rounded corners; everything is sharp, engraved-edge precision
* **Gradients as primary background treatments** — Art Deco backgrounds are flat, dark, and solid. The decorative richness comes from ornament, not from color gradients.
* **Condensed sans-serif body text** — body text must be readable and in a font with Art Deco credentials. Helvetica or Arial body text is incompatible.
* **Organic, flowing shapes as design elements** — Art Deco's organic references (peacock feathers, botanical elements) are always geometrically stylized. Pure organic forms are inconsistent.
* **More than two jewel accent colors** — the palette is disciplined. Gold + one jewel tone is the maximum.

## Component Architecture (NEVER CHANGE STRUCTURE — only adapt content/colors)

### A. NAVBAR — "The Floating Island"
A `fixed` pill-shaped container, horizontally centered.
- **Morphing Logic:** Transparent with light text at hero top. Transitions to `bg-[background]/60 backdrop-blur-xl` with primary-colored text and a subtle `border` when scrolled past the hero. Use `IntersectionObserver` or ScrollTrigger.
- Contains: Logo (brand name as text), 3-4 nav links, CTA button (accent color).

### B. HERO SECTION — "The Opening Shot"
- `100dvh` height. Full-bleed background image (sourced from Unsplash matching preset's `imageMood`) with a heavy **primary-to-black gradient overlay** (`bg-gradient-to-t`).
- **Layout:** Content pushed to the **bottom-left third** using flex + padding.
- **Typography:** Large scale contrast following the preset's hero line pattern. First part in bold sans heading font. Second part in massive serif italic drama font (3-5x size difference).
- **Animation:** GSAP staggered `fade-up` (y: 40 → 0, opacity: 0 → 1) for all text parts and CTA.
- CTA button below the headline, using the accent color.

### C. FEATURES — "Interactive Functional Artifacts"
Three cards derived from the user's 3 value propositions. These must feel like **functional software micro-UIs**, not static marketing cards. Each card gets one of these interaction patterns:

**Card 1 — "Diagnostic Shuffler":** 3 overlapping cards that cycle vertically using `array.unshift(array.pop())` logic every 3 seconds with a spring-bounce transition (`cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)`). Labels derived from user's first value prop (generate 3 sub-labels).

**Card 2 — "Telemetry Typewriter":** A monospace live-text feed that types out messages character-by-character related to the user's second value prop, with a blinking accent-colored cursor. Include a "Live Feed" label with a pulsing dot.

**Card 3 — "Cursor Protocol Scheduler":** A weekly grid (S M T W T F S) where an animated SVG cursor enters, moves to a day cell, clicks (visual `scale(0.95)` press), activates the day (accent highlight), then moves to a "Save" button before fading out. Labels from user's third value prop.

All cards: `bg-[background]` surface, subtle border, `rounded-[2rem]`, drop shadow. Each card has a heading (sans bold) and a brief descriptor.

### D. PHILOSOPHY — "The Manifesto"
- Full-width section with the **dark color** as background.
- A parallaxing organic texture image (Unsplash, `imageMood` keywords) at low opacity behind the text.
- **Typography:** Two contrasting statements. Pattern:
  - "Most [industry] focuses on: [common approach]." — neutral, smaller.
  - "We focus on: [differentiated approach]." — massive, drama serif italic, accent-colored keyword.
- **Animation:** GSAP `SplitText`-style reveal (word-by-word or line-by-line fade-up) triggered by ScrollTrigger.

### E. PROTOCOL — "Sticky Stacking Archive"
3 full-screen cards that stack on scroll.
- **Stacking Interaction:** Using GSAP ScrollTrigger with `pin: true`. As a new card scrolls into view, the card underneath scales to `0.9`, blurs to `20px`, and fades to `0.5`.
- **Each card gets a unique canvas/SVG animation:**
  1. A slowly rotating geometric motif (double-helix, concentric circles, or gear teeth).
  2. A scanning horizontal laser-line moving across a grid of dots/cells.
  3. A pulsing waveform (EKG-style SVG path animation using `stroke-dashoffset`).
- Card content: Step number (monospace), title (heading font), 2-line description. Derive from user's brand purpose.

### F. MEMBERSHIP / PRICING
- Three-tier pricing grid. Card names: "Essential", "Performance", "Enterprise" (adjust to fit brand).
- **Middle card pops:** Primary-colored background with an accent CTA button. Slightly larger scale or `ring` border.
- If pricing doesn't apply, convert this into a "Get Started" section with a single large CTA.

### G. FOOTER
- Deep dark-colored background, `rounded-t-[4rem]`.
- Grid layout: Brand name + tagline, navigation columns, legal links.
- **"System Operational" status indicator** with a pulsing green dot and monospace label.
