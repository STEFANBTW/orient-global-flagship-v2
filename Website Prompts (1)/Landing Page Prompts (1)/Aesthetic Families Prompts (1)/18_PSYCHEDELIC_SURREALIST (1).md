The Deterministic Application Guide: Psychedelic / Surrealist

* **WHY PSYCHEDELIC DESIGN WORKS** — THE PSYCHOLOGICAL FOUNDATION
Psychedelic and surrealist design works through deliberate perceptual disruption — it interrupts the automatic, habitual processing of visual information and forces conscious, active engagement. The human visual system is highly optimized for prediction — it constantly predicts what it will see next based on established patterns. Psychedelic design violates those predictions systematically.
The result is a state of heightened visual attention — the viewer cannot passively consume the design; they must actively process it. For brands whose product or service is itself an experience that disrupts normal perception (music, art, theatre, cannabis, experimental food), the design is therefore a perfect preview of the experience itself.
Surrealism specifically works through the juxtaposition of familiar objects in impossible contexts — referencing the Freudian unconscious where the rules of physical reality do not apply. When a viewer's brain processes a melting clock, it experiences the same cognitive dissonance it feels in a dream: familiar elements, impossible arrangement. This cognitive dissonance is itself fascinating — the brain keeps returning to the image, trying to resolve the impossibility.

### COLOR

#### The Palette Logic

Psychedelic color is built on three principles: maximum saturation, maximum contrast, and deliberate visual vibration.
* **Visual Vibration** — The Key Concept

Certain color combinations create an optical illusion of movement or vibration when placed adjacent to each other. This is because the human visual system processes different wavelengths (colors) at slightly different rates — when two highly saturated complementary colors meet at an edge, the visual system cannot stabilize that edge and it appears to shimmer. This is a biological effect, not a subjective impression.
The most vibrating color combinations:

* Red (`#FF0000`) adjacent to blue-green (`#00FFCC`) — near-complementary
* Electric purple (`#8B00FF`) adjacent to yellow-green (`#BFFF00`)
* Magenta (`#FF00FF`) adjacent to green (`#00FF00`) — direct complementary
Orange (`#FF8C00`) adjacent to deep blue (`#0040FF`)

Psychedelic design uses these vibrating pairs intentionally — the eye's discomfort at the boundary between the colors is part of the intended perceptual experience.
The Full Psychedelic Palette

Unlike other aesthetics that limit palette size, psychedelic design can use five to eight colors simultaneously. The key is that they must all be at or near maximum saturation:

* Electric purple: `#8B00FF`
* Hot orange: `#FF6600`
* Acid yellow-green: `#BFFF00`
* Magenta: `#FF00CC`
* Cyan: `#00FFFF`
* Coral red: `#FF3333`
* Royal blue: `#2244FF`

Background Options

* Pure black (`#000000`): makes every saturated color glow with maximum intensity — neon-like, electric
* Pure white (`#FFFFFF`): makes saturated colors feel more acidic and bright — the colors have a physical impact against the white ground
* Deep complementary-adjacent color: a very dark version of one of the palette colors (deep purple `#1A0033`, deep navy `#000533`) — creates a monochromatic depth that makes the brighter colors feel even more intense

### TYPOGRAPHY

The Core Philosophy

Psychedelic typography is the antithesis of Swiss rationalism. The letterform is not a neutral vehicle for information — it is itself a visual experience. The type melts, undulates, distorts, fills with patterns, wraps around shapes, and defies the conventional reading direction.

#### Font Classification

* **Path A** — The Psychedelic Lettering Font

Directly referencing the hand-lettered posters of 1960s San Francisco psychedelic rock:

Waltograph: fluid, ornamental, referencing Art Nouveau botanical forms blended with psychedelic lettering conventions
LSD: a font that literally has liquid-distortion built into its letterform shapes
Psychodelic: melting letterforms that appear to flow downward

* **Path B** — The Surrealist Juxtaposition

Taking a conventionally neutral typeface and using it in contexts or at scales that make it surreal:

Helvetica at 200px with a full-color psychedelic illustration filling the letterforms (background-clip: text technique)
Times New Roman tilted at 37 degrees with words overprinting each other
Any formal, expected typeface made strange through unexpected size, color, rotation, or context

* **Path C** — The Art Nouveau/Organic Revival

Letterforms that reference the flowing, botanical aesthetics of Art Nouveau — which the psychedelic movement consciously revived:

Jugendstil: ornate, organic letterforms
Fonts with calligraphic, fluid stroke qualities

#### Sizing Scale

* **Psychedelic design has no rational size scale** — size is expressive:

One word at 200px
The next word at 18px
The contrast between them is itself the visual experience
Body text (when used): 16px to 18px in a legible font — even psychedelic design has a moment of clarity for essential information

Spatial Typography

Text in psychedelic design is not set in lines:

Arranged in a spiral pattern radiating from a center point
Set to curve along the edge of a circular or organic shape
Individual letters at different rotations, distributed across the composition
* **Overprinting** — two words printed over each other in different colors, creating a composite that reads as neither word individually but both words simultaneously

### SPACING & LAYOUT

The Core Philosophy

Psychedelic design has no conventional layout logic. It references the visual experiences it is inspired by — where conventional spatial orientation is disrupted, where figure and ground can reverse, where depth is ambiguous.
Specific Layout Techniques
Figure-Ground Reversal: a composition where the positive space (the shapes) and the negative space (the background) are ambiguous — both read as possible figures. M.C. Escher's work is the most famous example. This is constructed using two colors at similar visual weights with interlocking shapes.
Impossible Depth: elements that appear to be in front of other elements based on overlap, but whose shadows or reflections indicate that they are behind — creating visual impossibility. References Dalí's use of trompe l'oeil illusion.
Radial Composition: elements arranged in concentric circles or spirals radiating from a center point — the visual structure of a kaleidoscope or a mandala.
Full-Surface Coverage: the entire visual surface (every pixel) is filled with pattern, color, or image. There is no "empty" space — even the background is a visual event.
Spacing Rule

There is no spacing rule. The design fills all available space with visual information. The conventional breathing room of other aesthetics is replaced with visual density — every area of the composition is active.

### BORDERS, SHAPES & FORMS

Organic, Impossible Shapes

Psychedelic design uses shapes that do not exist in conventional geometry:

Shapes that appear to be three-dimensional but have topological impossibilities (like a Möbius strip or a Klein bottle)
* **Organic shapes that appear to breathe or pulse** — blob forms that feel biologically alive
Mandala-like geometric forms where symmetry is absolute and patterns repeat fractally inward
Melting or drooping versions of normally rigid shapes (the Dalí clock)

#### Border Radius

Psychedelic design uses any and all border radius values — sometimes changing them dynamically (an element morphs from a square to a circle via CSS animation). The border radius is not a systematic choice — it is an expressive one.
Pattern as Surface

Surfaces in psychedelic design are not flat colors — they are patterns. Every background, every card surface, every button can be filled with:

Repeating psychedelic tile patterns
Gradient patterns cycling through the full spectrum
Optical illusion patterns (concentric circles, radial lines, checker distortions)

### IMAGERY & TEXTURE

#### Illustration

Psychedelic illustration is the most elaborate of all aesthetics:

Figurative surrealism: human figures combined with impossible anatomical features, or in impossible physical situations
Botanical/natural imagery pushed to surreal extremes — flowers with too many petals, trees that grow downward, plants with human faces
The face (specifically the eye) is the most common figurative element in psychedelic illustration — eyes within eyes, eyes in unexpected anatomical locations, multiple eyes arranged in geometric patterns
Architectural impossibility: staircases that loop back on themselves (referencing Escher), buildings that contain impossible interior spaces

Photography Treatment

Long-exposure photography: trails of light, blurred motion, color ghosts
Double exposure: two photographs superimposed, creating a composite image where both subjects are partially visible
Color manipulation at extremes: hue rotation, channel inversion, solarization (an extreme contrast effect that partly inverts tone)

Textures

Gradient textures cycling through the full color spectrum
Woven pattern overlays referencing textiles and tapestries
Marbling effects: the swirled patterns of marbled paper and oil-on-water
* **Extreme grain: at 30% to 50% opacity** — creating a physical, material surface quality

### ANIMATION & MOTION

Psychedelic animation is the most elaborate and perceptually demanding of all aesthetics:
Appropriate animations:

Slow morph: shapes slowly transform into other shapes — a circle gradually becomes a triangle over 4 to 8 seconds, then to a square, in a continuous loop
Color cycle: elements continuously cycle through the full color spectrum — the hue rotates 360 degrees over 4 to 6 seconds
Optical illusion animation: patterns that create the illusion of motion even though they are stationary — the peripheral drift illusion (concentric circles of alternating colors appear to spin when viewed peripherally)
* Liquid animation: text or shapes appear to melt or flow — achieved with CSS filter: url(#liquid-filter) using an SVG turbulence filter
Parallax at extreme scales: foreground elements move at very different speeds from background elements — creating dramatic, disorienting depth

What to strictly avoid:

* **Animations that resolve to a stable, calm state** — psychedelic animation is continuous, not momentary
* **Fast, snappy transitions** — psychedelic motion is slow and hypnotic
* **Functional UI state-change animations** — they break the perceptual immersion

WHAT TO STRICTLY AVOID IN PSYCHEDELIC DESIGN

* **Desaturated colors** — every color must be at or near maximum saturation. Muted colors are calm, and calm contradicts the aesthetic's purpose.
* **Rational, systematic layout** — a grid-based, ordered layout is perceptually comfortable, which is the opposite of the intended experience
* **Conventional typography** — body text may be conventional (for readability), but headline and display typography must be expressive and strange
* **Minimal content** — psychedelic design is dense. White space reads as empty, not luxurious.
* **Flat, solid-color surfaces** — every surface should have pattern, texture, or gradient movement
* **Corporate or institutional color associations** — no blue-grey palette, no neutral grey UI colors. Everything must be saturated and experiential.
* **Taking itself literally too seriously** — the surrealist tradition has humor and playfulness embedded in it. A psychedelic design that is earnest and grim has missed the cultural reference.

## Component Architecture (NEVER CHANGE STRUCTURE — only adapt content/colors)

### A. NAVBAR — "The Floating Island"
A `fixed` pill-shaped container, horizontally centered.
- **Morphing Logic:** Transparent with light text at hero top. Transitions to `bg-[background]/60 backdrop-blur-xl` with primary-colored text and a subtle `border` when scrolled past the hero. Use `IntersectionObserver` or ScrollTrigger.
- Contains: Logo (brand name as text), 3-4 nav links, CTA button (accent color).

### B. HERO SECTION — "The Opening Shot"
- `100dvh` height. Full-bleed background image (sourced from Unsplash matching preset's `imageMood`) with a heavy **primary-to-black gradient overlay** (`bg-gradient-to-t`).
- **Layout:** Content pushed to the **bottom-left third** using flex + padding.
- **Typography:** Large scale contrast following the preset's hero line pattern. First part in bold sans heading font. Second part in massive serif italic drama font (3-5x size difference).
- **Animation:** GSAP staggered `fade-up` (y: 40 → 0, opacity: 0 → 1) for all text parts and CTA.
- CTA button below the headline, using the accent color.

### C. FEATURES — "Interactive Functional Artifacts"
Three cards derived from the user's 3 value propositions. These must feel like **functional software micro-UIs**, not static marketing cards. Each card gets one of these interaction patterns:

**Card 1 — "Diagnostic Shuffler":** 3 overlapping cards that cycle vertically using `array.unshift(array.pop())` logic every 3 seconds with a spring-bounce transition (`cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)`). Labels derived from user's first value prop (generate 3 sub-labels).

**Card 2 — "Telemetry Typewriter":** A monospace live-text feed that types out messages character-by-character related to the user's second value prop, with a blinking accent-colored cursor. Include a "Live Feed" label with a pulsing dot.

**Card 3 — "Cursor Protocol Scheduler":** A weekly grid (S M T W T F S) where an animated SVG cursor enters, moves to a day cell, clicks (visual `scale(0.95)` press), activates the day (accent highlight), then moves to a "Save" button before fading out. Labels from user's third value prop.

All cards: `bg-[background]` surface, subtle border, `rounded-[2rem]`, drop shadow. Each card has a heading (sans bold) and a brief descriptor.

### D. PHILOSOPHY — "The Manifesto"
- Full-width section with the **dark color** as background.
- A parallaxing organic texture image (Unsplash, `imageMood` keywords) at low opacity behind the text.
- **Typography:** Two contrasting statements. Pattern:
  - "Most [industry] focuses on: [common approach]." — neutral, smaller.
  - "We focus on: [differentiated approach]." — massive, drama serif italic, accent-colored keyword.
- **Animation:** GSAP `SplitText`-style reveal (word-by-word or line-by-line fade-up) triggered by ScrollTrigger.

### E. PROTOCOL — "Sticky Stacking Archive"
3 full-screen cards that stack on scroll.
- **Stacking Interaction:** Using GSAP ScrollTrigger with `pin: true`. As a new card scrolls into view, the card underneath scales to `0.9`, blurs to `20px`, and fades to `0.5`.
- **Each card gets a unique canvas/SVG animation:**
  1. A slowly rotating geometric motif (double-helix, concentric circles, or gear teeth).
  2. A scanning horizontal laser-line moving across a grid of dots/cells.
  3. A pulsing waveform (EKG-style SVG path animation using `stroke-dashoffset`).
- Card content: Step number (monospace), title (heading font), 2-line description. Derive from user's brand purpose.

### F. MEMBERSHIP / PRICING
- Three-tier pricing grid. Card names: "Essential", "Performance", "Enterprise" (adjust to fit brand).
- **Middle card pops:** Primary-colored background with an accent CTA button. Slightly larger scale or `ring` border.
- If pricing doesn't apply, convert this into a "Get Started" section with a single large CTA.

### G. FOOTER
- Deep dark-colored background, `rounded-t-[4rem]`.
- Grid layout: Brand name + tagline, navigation columns, legal links.
- **"System Operational" status indicator** with a pulsing green dot and monospace label.
